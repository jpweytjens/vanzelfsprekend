# Locators

*Set your own ticks before or after `apply` or `range_frame` on a lone axes; the frame keeps them and still decides where the spine ends. Panels framed together take the frame's ticks at the call, so on those set your locator after it.*

To place ticks yourself (`QuartileLocator`, `FeatureLocator`, or any matplotlib locator) set it before or after `apply` or `range_frame`: on a lone axes the frame keeps whichever locator you installed, rather than overwriting it. Panels framed together, by `small_multiples` or by sharing an axis, are the exception: they compute a shared scale and take the frame's ticks at the call, so on those set your locator after it and it stands. A locator set that way reads its own panel's data, so its round numbers are its own while the scale stays shared, since the limits are pinned every draw by a separate step; the [grand tours](../tutorial/grand-tours.md) tick each race at its own extremes on one speed scale that way. The two take the same arguments, so everything here holds for either. The frame mode still decides where the spine ends, now reading your ticks, since [the frame follows the axes](../explanation/hook.md) at every draw: `inside` ends it at your outermost ticks within the data, `loose` and `flexible` at the ticks nearest the data at or beyond it, `data` at the data's extremes, and `feature` at your outermost marks, however far out they lie. That is how the [resonance figure](../tutorial/resonance-peak.md) holds its spine at 16–19 while the curve spills past.

Linear, log and date axes are handled; set the scale before calling `range_frame`, and plot date data first, since an axis only becomes a date axis once dates arrive on it. On a log axis the ticks sit on powers of the base and the minor ticks disappear with them; `ax.xaxis.set_minor_locator(matplotlib.ticker.LogLocator(subs="auto"))` brings the minors back. On a date axis the ticks sit on calendar starts (a year, a month, a day, an hour) and the labels shorten to what changes between ticks, so a run of months does not repeat the year. That shared year sits at the right end of the bottom spine, where `xlabel` goes, and stacks above an `xlabel` if you set one. The calendar steps the locator chooses among are not exposed; for a fixed step, set one of matplotlib's date locators before or after `apply`, `mdates.YearLocator(10)` say, and the frame reads it. Anything else (`symlog`, `logit`, categorical axes) is left untouched with a warning. The warning covers the frame and ticks only; `line_labels` places its labels on any scale.

The locators that ship with the library each answer one question. `QuartileLocator` ticks the minimum, quartiles and maximum, the five-number summary, as in the Anscombe figure. `SummaryLocator` ticks any reducers of one axis's values, the slowest, median and fastest winner in the grand tours. `FeatureLocator` ticks features of an x, y pair, a peak's own frequency in the resonance figure. `TalbotLocator`, `LogBreaksLocator` and `DateBreaksLocator` are the defaults `range_frame` installs for linear, log and date axes, and can be used on their own with any matplotlib axes. The [reference](../reference/locators.md) has every argument.

`QuartileLocator`, `SummaryLocator` and `FeatureLocator` replace the round-number ticks with yours. To keep the round numbers and add a few values among them, wrap the frame's locator in `AugmentedLocator`:

```python
ax.vzs.apply()
base = ax.yaxis.get_major_locator()
ax.yaxis.set_major_locator(vzs.AugmentedLocator(base, [8.51, 8.76]))
```

Build it after `apply`, from the locator the frame installed. Built before, it would wrap matplotlib's default locator, which ticks the view rather than the data, and the frame would keep that. The added values can be a list or any of the fixed locators above, so a `FeatureLocator` brings its named features along, and [`accent`](accent.md) picks the added ticks out by value or by name. Under `frame="feature"` the spine runs between the added values and leaves the round numbers to the ticks. The [labour-income tutorial](../tutorial/labour-income.md) adds a component's two recovered levels, a tick at zero, and the months holiday pay and a bonus recur in this way.
